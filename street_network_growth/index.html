Street network growth

A model of how a city's street network grows, one street at a time. A new street touches between 1 and E others. Some are side streets built with it (a share f of all contacts); the rest are older streets, and each of them gains a contact. A street that touches two or more is at most (E − 1) / 3 block sides long, and with chance q a new group of streets turns to a new angle.

What a step does

A new street draws its birth degree B, uniform from 1 to E. Each of its B − 1 other contacts becomes a side street with chance p; the rest must be streets that already exist. It is placed at a random point, moved to the middle of its strip and run both ways until it has met them. Then its side streets are built on it, one after the other, each with its own B, and so on. Nothing else is built until the development is done.

What f is

A junction between two streets of the same development is an internal contact: a birth contact for both streets, and no older street gains anything. A junction with a street that was already there is a contact with an existing street: that old street gains +1.

f is the share of all birth contacts that are internal. The model turns it into a chance per contact:

p = f (E + 1) / (2 (E − 1))

Old streets grow only through the +1s, so the larger f, the steeper the tail of p(K) above E. In the mean field the exponent is α = 2 + 1 / (1 − f).

What q is

The first street of a development takes a frame. With chance q the frame is a new angle, uniform; otherwise it is the angle of the street nearest the point the street starts from. The street runs along or across its frame, and its side streets run at right angles to it, so each development is a small grid in its own frame. At q = 0 every street is at 0 or 90 degrees.

Streets meet at 30 degrees or more, so DGDC keeps them apart. q leaves the degrees and α alone and sets the detour: a trip at an angle to the grid gains from streets in other frames.

What the cap is

A street that touches two or more streets when it is placed may be at most

(E − 1) ℓ / 3

long, with ℓ the mean block side so far, however many streets it touches. That is ℓ at E = 4, the cities' elbow. A street with many contacts must go where streets are dense, so length stays flat up to the elbow. Dead ends are not capped.

Scaling the cap with E leaves a street that touches E streets a third of a block side per gap at every E, so the elbow stays at E.
